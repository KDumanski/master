## Resonance & the human body

### Schumann Resonance (7.8 Hz) and human physiology

**Why I care:** The connection between natural environmental resonance and human brain waves. The Earth has a background electromagnetic "hum" — the Schumann Resonance — whose fundamental frequency (7.8 Hz) sits right inside the alpha brain-wave band (8–12 Hz). The question that pulls me in: is there a real, measurable coupling between the frequency the planet rings at and the frequency our nervous system runs on, or is the overlap a coincidence?

**Source paper:**
Mayrovitz, Harvey N. *"Impact of a Generated Magnetic Field at the Schumann Resonance Frequency on Skin Blood Perfusion and Peripheral Pulse Amplitudes."* Cureus, April 29, 2025.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12121975/

**What the study actually did (faithful summary):**
- A small pilot study — 6 young male volunteers (avg age ~26), each serving as his own control.
- They generated an artificial magnetic field oscillating at 7.8 Hz (the Schumann fundamental) and applied it locally to the wrist for 20 minutes, after a 20-minute rest baseline.
- Field strength was ~1300 Gauss at the device's surface.
- They measured three things: skin blood perfusion (laser Doppler), finger pulse amplitude (photoplethysmography), and heart rate variability (EKG).

**What they found:**
- Skin blood perfusion dropped ~24% after exposure (statistically significant).
- Finger pulse amplitude dropped ~36% after exposure (statistically significant).
- Heart rate variability showed no significant change.
- The effect looked local (at the exposure site), not systemic.

**Important caveats (the authors say so themselves):**
- Tiny sample, all young males.
- No sham/placebo control — so we can't fully separate the field's effect from rest, posture, or other drift.
- Wrist anatomy varied between subjects.
- They frame it explicitly as groundwork for a bigger, properly controlled study — not a settled result.

**The honest gap between this paper and my interest:**
This study is about *blood flow in the skin*, not brain waves. It does not measure EEG or anything neural. So it is **not** evidence that 7.8 Hz resonance entrains brain activity. What it is: a hint that human tissue can respond to a magnetic field oscillating at that specific frequency at all — which is the first domino. The brain-wave link is still my own hypothesis, not something this paper demonstrates.

**Where this thread could go (next questions to chase):**
- Is there real EEG research on whether 7.8 Hz external fields shift alpha activity? (Look for "magnetic field EEG entrainment," "alpha entrainment," "brainwave entrainment Schumann.")
- Sham-controlled replications of this Mayrovitz study — does the blood-flow effect survive a placebo control?
- The mechanism question: the authors guess it's about sympathetic tone / vasoconstriction. Is the frequency actually special, or would any low-frequency field do the same? (Need a dose/frequency-response study to know if 7.8 Hz is meaningful or arbitrary.)
- The big-picture framing: separate the *measured science* (weak fields can produce small local physiological effects) from the *speculative leap* (the planet's resonance "tunes" the brain). Keep those two clearly apart so the idea stays grounded.
